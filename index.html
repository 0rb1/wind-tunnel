Anatomy of a real wind tunnel

Welcome! Before you run your first experiment, meet the machine. A wind tunnel doesn't move the aircraft: it moves the air, and holds the model still. This diagram shows a closed-circuit tunnel, the kind used by NASA and universities: air loops around continuously, driven by a fan. Everything you'll simulate on this page happens in one place: the test section (highlighted).

1Fan & drive: a giant propeller pushes the air around the loop. Big tunnels use fans the size of houses driven by megawatt motors.
2Turning vanes: curved blades in each corner steer the air smoothly around the bend so it doesn't tumble.
3Settling chamber & honeycomb: screens and honeycomb straighten the flow, ironing out swirl and turbulence before the measurement.
4Contraction cone: the duct narrows, so the air must speed up (conservation of mass). This also flattens out any remaining unevenness.
5Test section, you are here! the fastest, cleanest air meets the model. Everything this simulator shows happens in this glass box.
6Diffuser: the duct widens gently, slowing the air to recover pressure so the fan needs less power.
